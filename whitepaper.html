The Problem

Traditional self-custody wallets have two fundamental vulnerabilities. In 2024, personal wallet compromises grew to 44% of all stolen crypto — up from 7% in 2022. Over 80,000 unique victims lost $713M in the first half of 2025 alone.

Device-Level Attacks

$1.2B lost in 2024

All standard wallets store encrypted seed phrases locally on your device. Malware, compromised backups, or physical access to the file system exposes the encrypted data. In 2024, private-key exploits caused $1.2 billion in losses across 47 attacks — 69% of all crypto theft in early 2025.

Weak Password Protection

$438M+ drained (LastPass breach)

With standard encryption, a wrong password produces gibberish — so an attacker knows instantly when they have guessed correctly. The 2022 LastPass breach exposed encrypted vaults, and brute-forcing weak master passwords has drained $438M+ through 2025 — including $150M from Ripple co-founder Chris Larsen.

The W2QR Solution

W2QR eliminates both attack surfaces by removing secrets from devices entirely and making password guessing mathematically pointless.

Honey Encryption

Every password — right or wrong — produces a valid BIP-39 mnemonic with real wallet addresses. An attacker trying a million passwords gets a million plausible wallets. There is no way to tell which one is real without checking every derived address on the blockchain.

Brute-force cost: checking each candidate requires querying multiple blockchains across multiple derivation paths — making large-scale attacks economically infeasible.

Mnemonic-Based Strong Password

Choose an easy-to-remember phrase in any language — for example, "I was born in London, and my whole family is from that same area." W2QR derives a cryptographic password from it. The generated password is long and complex, but you never need to memorize it — store it as a password QR code that you scan when opening your wallet.

Estimated brute-force time: ~10⁴⁵ years on a powerful PC. Even a hypothetical quantum computer would need ~175 billion years. If you ever lose the password QR, just regenerate it from the same phrase — the derivation is deterministic.

Zero Footprint on Devices

No app install required. Open your wallet in any browser with your QR + password. All encryption and decryption runs client-side. No data remains on the device after locking — nothing to find, nothing to steal.

No encrypted files on disk, no localStorage secrets, no cached keys. The wallet exists only in browser memory while unlocked.

Safe Anywhere Backup

Print your QR, save it as an image, or back it up to Google Drive. The QR is safe to store anywhere — even publicly — because without your mnemonic password, brute-force is futile.

Google Drive backup + memorable mnemonic password = reliable access that is immune to device loss, theft, or failure.

Plastic Cards for Both QR Codes

Print your wallet QR and your password QR on credit-card-sized plastic cards. Two cards, two locations — wallet access is as simple as scanning both. Even if someone finds one card, it is useless without the other: honey encryption blocks the wallet card, and the password card alone opens nothing.

Keep them separate: wallet card in one safe place, password card in another. Together they provide friendly, physical access to your crypto. Apart, each is worthless to an attacker.

Power desktop PC: high-end GPU cluster at 10 billion guesses/sec. Quantum computer: Grover's algorithm (quadratic speedup) on a hypothetical large-scale quantum computer. W2QR also uses Argon2 memory-hard KDF, adding ~1 second per attempt regardless of hardware.

Security Model

  • All encryption/decryption runs client-side in the browser — the server never sees your password, mnemonic, or private keys
  • No password verifier is stored anywhere — there is nothing for an attacker to validate guesses against
  • Memory-hard key derivation (Argon2) makes each password attempt computationally expensive
  • Honey encryption ensures every decryption attempt produces a valid-looking result
  • Zero data persistence on the device after wallet lock
  • Optional key for additional entropy on top of the password
