W2QR Security Architecture

Security & Recovery

Three layers of protection make your wallet virtually unhackable. Two recovery paths ensure you never lose access.

Part A

Why your wallet is secure

  1. Honey encryption eliminates brute force

    With traditional encryption, a wrong password produces gibberish — so an attacker knows immediately when a guess is wrong. W2QR uses honey encryption: every password, right or wrong, produces a valid BIP‑39 seed phrase with real wallet addresses.

    An attacker who steals your QR code and tries a million passwords gets a million plausible wallets. There is no “wrong password” error, no hash to check, no way to reject a guess locally. The only way to find the real wallet is to derive addresses from each candidate mnemonic and check them across multiple blockchains and derivation paths.

    Combined with Argon2id key derivation (64 MB memory, 3 iterations), each attempt costs ~1 second regardless of hardware. Brute-forcing billions of passwords and verifying each one on-chain is economically infeasible.

  2. Strong password from your memorable phrase

    Choose a phrase you can easily remember in any language — for example, “I was born in London, and my whole family is from that same area.” The Strong Password Generator transforms it into a cryptographic password with over 150 bits of entropy.

    Estimated brute-force time on a powerful GPU cluster: ~1045 years. Even a hypothetical large-scale quantum computer using Grover’s algorithm would need ~175 billion years — far beyond the age of the universe.

    The generated password is long, complex, and impossible to memorize — but you don’t have to. Store it as a password QR code: print it, and use it by scanning. You remember your phrase. The QR carries the password.

  3. Plastic cards: friendly access for both QR codes

    Print your wallet QR and your password QR on plastic cards — credit-card-sized, durable, easy to carry and store. Two cards, two locations: wallet access is as simple as scanning both.

    Even if someone finds your wallet card, honey encryption means they cannot tell which password unlocks it. And without your password card, they would have to brute-force a 150+ bit password — effectively impossible.

    Keep them separate: your wallet card in one safe place, your password card in another. Together they open your wallet. Apart, each is useless to an attacker.

Part B

How you recover access

  1. Google Drive backup for your wallet QR

    Back up your encrypted wallet QR to Google Drive. The backup is ciphertext — Google stores the file but cannot read your password, seed phrase, or private keys. If you lose the printed QR or the plastic card, restore it from Drive in seconds.

    The backup is identical to the original QR data. Restoring it is like scanning your printed QR — you still need your password to open the wallet.

  2. Password recovery via your memorable phrase

    If your strong password was generated from a memorable phrase, you can regenerate it at any time. Open the Strong Password Generator, type the same phrase, and you get the same password — it is a deterministic derivation, not a random process.

    This means your password QR is also recoverable: regenerate the password from your phrase, then create a new password QR. No backup file needed — your memory of the phrase is the backup.

What W2QR never has access to

  • Your password
  • Your seed phrase
  • Your private keys
  • Your wallet address

This is intentional

W2QR has no “reset password” button, no recovery email, and no customer support override. Your wallet cannot be accessed by W2QR, by Google, or by anyone without your QR and password. This means no one can take your funds — including us — but it also means you must protect your own credentials.

One sentence to remember

Your wallet = your QR + a password derived from a memorable phrase in your native language. Back up the QR to Google Drive, remember your phrase, and you can always recover.
